The problem

Generative AI can fake a flawless product photo, a handwritten note, even a shipping label. The picture is no longer evidence.

When buyers judge an item from a single image in seconds, AI-synthesized listings, stolen photos and re-shot screens are nearly impossible to tell apart. The question has shifted — from "does this look real?" to "when, where and how was this actually captured?"

The shift

We move authentication from the person to the object — and from detecting the result to proving the process.

The old way · detection

Guess if the photo is fake

Platforms race to classify suspicious posts, chats and images. But detection is a probability game that AI keeps winning — and it can never promise "this object exists, right now."

Memo-note selfies · "send more photos" · ruler next to item — all forgeable.
ProofShot · proof

Prove the capture happened

Instead of judging the output, we make evidence that can only exist when a real person photographs a real object, live, on random demand — leaving a record no pre-made image can satisfy.

How it works

The seller shoots. The buyer checks a link.
No new app, no new platform.

In-app live capture

Only photos taken inside the app count. Gallery uploads are blocked, so stolen and re-used images can't enter.

Capture record anchored

A unique hash of the original file is sealed at the moment of capture and anchored into the Bitcoin blockchain via OpenTimestamps.

Verification link generated

A public page anyone can open — no install required — showing the proof behind the listing.

Badge on the listing

A copy-paste "Real-item verified" badge and link drop into any channel: Danggeun, Bunjang, Joonggonara, open chats, Instagram.

Buyer confirms before paying

One tap shows capture time, originality and verification status — turning doubt into a decision.

Live demo

Create a proof in seconds. Hashed and sealed right in your browser.

Shoot inside the app and the capture time, device and original SHA-256 hash are sealed — then the hash is anchored into the Bitcoin blockchain via OpenTimestamps, generating a verification link you can drop into any listing. No upload, no account; your photo stays on the device.

This demo is not a simulation. Every capture below runs the real anchoring pipeline — its hash is actually submitted to public OpenTimestamps calendar servers and committed to the Bitcoin blockchain, and the resulting .ots proof can be verified independently by anyone.
